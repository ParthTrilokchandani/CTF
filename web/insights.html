Insights

Occasional commentary from our research team.

Why Diversified Due Diligence Beats a Single Source

A recurring theme in post-mortem reviews of failed due diligence is over-reliance on a single data source or screening tool. Automated screening is a useful first pass, but it is not a substitute for corroboration. In our experience, findings that rely on exactly one source are wrong or incomplete often enough that a second, independent source should be treated as a minimum standard rather than a luxury.

Reading Political Risk Without Overreacting to Headlines

Clients often ask us to comment on a single news event as though it were, on its own, a reliable signal of changing risk. Our approach is the opposite: we look for confirmation across multiple, independent indicators before revising a risk assessment, and we are explicit with clients about the difference between a genuine shift and normal background noise.

The Cost of Delayed Counterparty Reviews

Periodic re-screening of existing counterparties is frequently the first thing cut from a compliance budget, and it is one of the more expensive line items to skip. Relationships that were clean at onboarding do not necessarily stay that way, particularly over multi-year engagements. We recommend a fixed re-screening cadence rather than an ad hoc one triggered only by external news.

What We Mean by "Assessment," Not "Prediction"

We are occasionally asked to predict a specific future outcome with a level of confidence the underlying evidence simply does not support. Our reports are structured to separate confirmed fact from informed assessment, and to flag explicitly where evidence is genuinely mixed rather than force a false sense of certainty for the sake of a cleaner-sounding conclusion.
